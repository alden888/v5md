# Factory vs. Trading Company vs. Integrator: The 2025 Sourcing Guide

> **Executive Summary**
> For decades, medical buyers faced a dilemma: Go direct to **Factories** for the lowest price (but suffer high MOQs and logistical headaches), or use **Trading Companies** for convenience (but pay hidden markups and lose QA control).
>
> In 2025, a third option has matured: **The Supply Chain Integrator**. This article analyzes the three models side-by-side and explains why the Integrator model is the most resilient strategy for post-pandemic procurement.

---

## The Traditional Dilemma

Every procurement manager knows the trade-off. You want to source Surgical Gowns, Sutures, and Syringes from China. You have two traditional options:

### Option A: Direct to Factory (The "Hard Way")
You find three separate factories. One for gowns, one for sutures, one for syringes.
* **The Gain:** You get the "Ex-Works" (EXW) factory price. Cheapest on paper.
* **The Pain:**
    * **High MOQs:** You must buy a full container of *just* gowns to get that price.
    * **Logistics:** You now manage 3 shipments, 3 customs declarations, and 3 payments.
    * **Inconsistency:** Factory A has ISO 13485; Factory B does not. You are the quality police.

### Option B: The Trading Company (The "Black Box")
You hire a trading company to source everything.
* **The Gain:** One contact person, lower MOQs.
* **The Pain:**
    * **Price Padding:** They add 15-30% margin, often hiding the original factory identity.
    * **Zero QA Control:** They are salespeople, not engineers. If the factory ships bad goods, the trader often finds out only *after* you receive them.
    * **Risk:** If the trader disappears, you lose your supply chain.

### How the Integrator Model Wins

#### 1. "Virtual Factory" Agility
We don't own the injection molding machines, but we own the **Quality Process**. We contract manufacturing to specialized facilities (e.g., a top-tier needle factory) but enforce **V5's Release Criteria**.
* **Benefit:** You get "Factory Direct" pricing structures without being tied to one factory's limited catalog.

#### 2. Consolidated Logistics (The Money Saver)
This is the biggest hidden saving.
* **Scenario:** You need $15,000 of Sutures + $20,000 of PVC Tubing.
* **Factory Model:** Two LCL (Less than Container Load) shipments. High port fees, high risk of damage.
* **Integrator Model:** V5 consolidates these into one 20ft container.
* **Result:** You save **~15-20% on landed cost** just by optimizing logistics, even if the unit price is slightly higher than the raw factory cost.

#### 3. Regulatory Shielding
In 2025, with EU MDR and strict FDA audits, "hiding" the manufacturer is illegal.
* **V5 Approach:** We provide full transparency. We hold the Technical Files. We manage the CE/ISO certifications. We act as the legal exporter, ensuring your compliance paper trail is perfect.
